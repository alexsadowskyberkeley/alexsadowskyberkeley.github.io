Augmented spark torch igniter

I lead the team building a spark torch igniter for STAR's liquid rocket engine. This is the first augmented spark igniter the club has attempted, so there was no earlier design to start from. The trade study, the plumbing diagram and the hardware are all new work. I own the injector faceplate and its connector.

My role

  • Ran the trade study that selected the propellants, chamber pressure and O/F ratio, and presented it to the team. It compared methane, ethanol, propane and hydrogen with gaseous oxygen, screening each in NASA CEA before scoring handling, storage and availability.
  • Developed the ground support P&ID with ISA symbology, valve sequencing and nitrogen purge logic to prevent hard starts.
  • Designing the injector faceplate in Onshape so it mates with the propellant valves and teammates' parts, and can be machined manually in our shop.

Constraints I design to

  • Parts are machined manually in the team's shop, so features have to be simple to set up and cut.
  • The geometry has to meet the FAR-OUT competition requirements.
  • The team is on a short deadline, with no previous igniter design or test data to build on.

Driver assistance prototype

A box that retrofits onto any car and tells the driver when to speed up or slow down to keep traffic flowing. An ultrasonic sensor measures the gap to the car ahead, an Arduino estimates that car's speed, and a row of red, yellow and green LEDs gives the cue. The driver stays in control the whole time.

My role

  • Designed the chassis and sensor housings in SolidWorks.
  • Refined tolerances through iterative prototyping and 3D printing.
  • Led hardware integration and ran hand calculations to check the structure.

Result

  • Working test car that reads following distance and the lead car's speed in real time.
  • Presented at Berkeley's Design Expo and earned Best Prototype.

Curtain pulley system

A wall-mounted pulley and hand-cranked geared spool that opens and closes a curtain from a spot away from the window, for rooms where furniture blocks the curtain. Six printed parts, held together with press-fit pins.

My role

  • Drew the assembly drawings: both exploded views with bills of materials, and both outline assemblies.
  • Diagnosed the fit classes for the fits table, RC8 running fits where parts rotate and LC7 fits for the pins.
  • Measured printed parts and computed the mean, standard deviation and standard error to check them against the drawings.
  • Assembled the prototype, then watched another group build it from our drawings alone.

What the build taught us

  • Early prints had gears that would not mesh and clearances that were too tight or too loose. We adjusted the CAD and reprinted until the mechanism ran smoothly.
  • The printer's accuracy of about 0.1 to 0.2 mm shaped most of the tolerance choices.

Mold for a carbon fiber seat

A personal project to learn composite tooling. I started from an existing race car seat model and worked out how to make it in carbon fiber: what to change on the part, how to design and split the mold, how to machine it on a 3-axis router, and how to lay it up. The mold is fully programmed and simulated. It has not been machined yet.

What I did

  • Design for manufacturing. Ran draft analysis on the seat and removed what a 3-axis mold cannot form: two slots, the wing holes, a backward-curling lip and an overhanging rim. The slots and holes get cut after cure.
  • Mold design. Designed a two-piece male plug with a 15 degree skirt, a molded-in crease that marks the trim line, a 100 mm flange for bagging, and three 16 mm dowels to locate the halves.
  • CAM. Programmed three setups in Fusion 360 with two tools: adaptive roughing, scallop finishing with a ball mill, and a flat-end contour pass for the foot of the walls where the ball cannot reach. Simulated run time is 4 hours 44 minutes.
  • Layup plan. Planned a resin infusion with 200 gsm twill at 0/90 and ±45 around a 3 mm PET core, with the consumable stack, resin inlet and vacuum outlets laid out on the mold.

What I got wrong first

  • I designed a female cavity mold first. The cutter and holder could not reach into a cavity that deep on three axes, so I rebuilt it as a male plug. That cost two days.
  • I resisted splitting the mold. Measuring showed the split drops the tallest piece from 249.8 mm to 170.0 mm, well under the router's 304.8 mm gantry clearance.
  • I planned a wet layup, then switched to infusion, which changed the core. A solid foam core blocks resin from the plies beneath it, so I moved to a PET core with flow channels.
  • I set the finishing boundary at the tool center, which left the steepest walls uncut. Moving it outside the part fixed it.

I spent the summer of 2026 as a manufacturing engineering intern on high-volume production lines. I owned four projects. Each one started with a loss on the line and ended with a documented fix.

5.8 → 0stops per day

Top-loss PLC fault on the log saw

Problem
A recurring controller fault was one of the line's biggest sources of lost production, stopping the log saw several times a day.
What I did
Led a 5 Why investigation with electrical engineers. I trended the fault data down to the millisecond and read the port counters on the Ethernet card, which pointed to a misconfigured Ethernet port.
Result
We corrected the port. I tracked stops before and after, and they went from 5.8 a day to zero.
25%fewer hygiene-related losses

Rebuilt cleaning standard for the unwind stand

Problem
Hygiene-related losses on the unwind stand machine kept recurring under the existing cleaning standard.
What I did
Rebuilt the cleaning execution standard as a continuous improvement project. I ran the cleaning myself to prove the new steps worked, did a time study with operators, and wrote a training plan.
Result
Certified more than 12 operators on the new standard. Hygiene-related losses fell by 25%.

Automated equipment scorecard

Problem
Equipment owners assembled a monthly scorecard by hand, which took over a workday per line.
What I did
Built an interactive Power BI dashboard on SQL-based Databricks pipelines. It tracks defects found, fixed and still open, along with unplanned stops. Along the way I found and flagged data integrity issues in the source system.

Chronic hydraulic leak

Problem
A hydraulic leak kept coming back, creating a fire and slip hazard.
What I did
Led a fishbone investigation. An absorbent mat test ruled out the hoses and fittings, so I checked the seal against the manufacturer's drawings and bill of materials and found the seal material was out of spec.
Result
Wrote the corrective and preventive action as a work instruction, which other production lines then adopted.
